# Reach: market SWOT and proposed requirements

Research date: 13 September 2026. Scope: affordable communication for home care, hospital care, and family relationships, including people with very limited movement.

This is a broad market landscape covering 19 product families/categories, not an exhaustive inventory of every regional app, device variant, or discontinued product. Product facts come from public vendor/project documentation; no comparative usability benchmark was performed. A separate, narrow AsTeRICS file-transfer check is documented in [the interoperability notes](artifacts/ASTERICS-INTEROP.md); that is not an assessment of communication effectiveness. Weaknesses describe fit for Reach's intended users, not universal defects. Opportunities and threats are strategic judgments. A missing documented feature is not proof that a product lacks it. Prices below are advertised USD software prices observed during research, excluding devices, tax and regional differences. Dedicated-device costs and funding eligibility require individual quotes; the earlier $13k–$18k estimate is not reused as a verified market price.

## Recommendation

Position Reach as a free, browser-based way to begin communicating quickly with the movement a person can reliably make today. Differentiate through recovery from input problems, low caregiver workload, adult-appropriate communication, and continuity between home and hospital.

Free boards and webcam interaction already exist. The strongest opportunity is to combine them into an independently usable care workflow, with evidence that real people can select, correct and complete messages. Do not position the current experimental webcam estimator as equivalent to a dedicated eye tracker.

For this app, prioritize the complete journey from opening the page to expressing an intended message, repairing an error and resuming after a break. Compare against AsTeRICS for free browser AAC, Speech Assistant for adult conversation, Grid/Voco Chat for access and partner support, and printed care boards for dependable backup. These are complementary benchmarks; there is no single best system for every person's movement, literacy, vision and language.

## Decisions for the next release

1. **Finish and validate independent access before adding more camera modes (R01–R04, R07, R19).** Test setup, timing adjustments, navigation, correction and recovery using only the chosen input. Record every helper intervention. Include repositioning, glasses, lighting changes and tired sessions. Large gaze zones and step-by-step calibration should be optional experiments, not an assumed cure for unreliable tracking. Grid already provides configurable calibration, feedback and activation methods, so this is a usability requirement rather than a unique feature. [Grid eye settings](https://hub.thinksmartbox.com/knowledgebase/eye-gaze-settings-in-grid-3/).
2. **Make communication usable without reading a dense grid (R09, R18).** Personal photos, a small selectable symbol library and optional auditory scanning now exist. Expand and validate these with users: check symbol recognition, preview comprehension, adjustable prompt pace and repeat. Keep preview prompts distinct from committed speech and preserve learned button positions. [Voco Chat](https://thinksmartbox.com/voco-chat/), [Speech Assistant](https://asoft.nl/).
3. **Finish language support end to end (R09–R10).** Translate setup, recovery and error messages as well as phrase tiles; provide native-speaker review and a practical Chinese composition route. Independently select the communicator's language, partner display and output voice. Test absent voices and long/RTL text. Language count alone should not determine readiness.
4. **Make failure survivable (R06, R11, R20).** Verify audible offline restart on inexpensive physical devices, media-inclusive recovery on a second browser, and removal of a person's data on shared equipment. Keep a printable communication passport and backup board available. An offline badge must not imply that a speech voice is available.
5. **Finish open transfer (R12).** Single-board OBF export and import code now exist; complete supported-format round trips, media/attribution preservation and clear handling of unsupported actions. OBZ and linked boards remain separate work. Do not rebuild existing export or describe partially implemented transfer as complete.
6. **Keep ownership and affordability explicit (R21).** Essential speech, access methods, local audio, backups and export should remain free and ad-free. Publish asset licensing and a reproducible build. Count the cost of the device, mount, connectivity and helper time when evaluating affordability, not only the app price.

Defer remote messaging and AI until these foundations work in real use. Remote requests need explicit delivery/acknowledgement states; local speech is not a delivered alert. AI may offer editable suggestions, but it must never decide what the person meant or automatically speak a generated answer.

## Reach's own SWOT

**Strengths:** no-account browser workflow; free local-first core; existing touch, dwell, switch and experimental camera options; large selection feedback; six-language core phrases; three care/family presets; printable board and JSON transfer.

**Weaknesses:** real-person camera usability unverified; physical-device coverage incomplete; some setup still needs assistance; advanced localization incomplete; limited personal visual media and vocabulary; incomplete open-format interoperability and no remote acknowledgement workflow. Composition, care handover, profiles, recordings, bilingual display and single-board OBF transfer now have initial implementations, but their presence does not establish independent usability. New Chinese text still needs a device IME. Browser storage and installed speech voices remain dependencies.

**Opportunities:** rapid temporary communication during care, affordable family use, continuity as movement changes, bilingual patient/partner display, and collaboration with existing open AAC tools. An accessible recovery path could matter more than adding another tracking algorithm.

**Threats:** established vendors have training/support ecosystems; free competitors already have broader vocabularies; OS accessibility can outperform a web estimator for some users; browser changes can break camera/speech; failures or wrongly spoken selections can destroy trust.

## Proposed requirements

P0 = next release foundation; P1 = subsequent care/family release; P2 = later validated extension. Acceptance checks below are proposed engineering/usability targets, not achieved results or clinical standards.

| ID | Priority | Requirement and concrete acceptance check | Competitive lesson / current gap |
|---|---|---|---|
| R01 | P0 | **Ability-led setup.** Ask how the person can select, how they understand messages, their language and current position. Try large known targets and record success, false selections, time and helper actions. Offer a simpler method when a trial fails, without losing phrases. | OS tools, Grid and AsTeRICS show that access must fit the person. Existing Reach setup chooses a mode but does not establish real-world suitability. |
| R02 | P0 | **No-drag daily navigation.** All normal communication pages fit the viewport at the selected target size; use large fixed Next/Back/Home. Retain scrolling for long setup panels. Complete a preset conversation without dragging, tiny menus or hidden controls. | Current scroll dock helps, but pagination removes moving-target demands. |
| R03 | P0 | **Selection correction and user control.** Persistent “Wrong selection,” “Stop speaking,” “Wait,” and “I don't know.” Offer configurable preview/confirm for users who need it, without forcing double effort on every message. Cancellation must remove queued speech; a spoken mistake needs an explicit correction, since it cannot be unsaid. | Mature text AAC supports message preparation; current dwell can voice a mistaken selection immediately. |
| R04 | P0 | **Reliable fallback and rest.** Preserve vocabulary while moving to two/four choices, switch scanning or partner-assisted scanning. Freeze selection on tracking loss; provide an independently reachable rest control. Do not silently change layouts or input methods. Verify recovery after camera loss and deliberate repositioning. | Dedicated access workflows and paper boards. Reach already gates tracking loss, but needs a complete recovery workflow. |
| R05 | P0 | **Stable, editable phrase packs.** Pin essential words and preserve their positions. Caregiver/user can hide or add messages without automatic frequency-based reordering. Every pack includes disagreement, uncertainty and correction. Verify positions survive restart, language changes and pack editing. | Motor-planning approaches and robust AAC; 12 predefined messages alone are restrictive. |
| R06 | P0 | **Provable offline readiness.** A readiness screen separately checks board, selected camera assets and audible speech. Test airplane-mode restart on each supported device. Add local recorded-audio fallback for essential phrases when a language voice is missing; show exact download sizes. | Cboard/AsTeRICS and device voice dependencies. Existing cache status does not prove a usable voice. |
| R07 | P0 | **Human usability validation.** Trial with AAC users and care partners across all three settings and input modes; involve AAC/SLP expertise. Measure completed messages, unintended selections per minute, correction time, fatigue and setup assistance. Include glasses, varied lighting, reclined positions and low-cost devices. Publish failures and supported configurations. | Vendor capabilities are not comparative clinical evidence; synthetic landmark tests are insufficient. |
| R08 | P1 | **Structured care messages.** Large-choice body location, optional severity and time, plus “not sure.” Build “My left shoulder hurts since this morning” from explicit selections and offer a final review. Do not infer symptoms, diagnosis or treatment. | Care boards support a conversation beyond “pain.” |
| R09 | P1 | **Family identity and unrestricted expression.** Personal photos, names, recorded voice, humor, opinions and stories; accessible spelling/phrase composition. “Something else” must always lead somewhere useful. Keep private content out of shared presets unless chosen. | Speech Assistant, Proloquo4Text and Weave Chat. Presets should be shortcuts, not limits on what a person can say. |
| R10 | P1 | **Bilingual partner display.** Separate user's display language, spoken language and partner translation. Show both versions together with large text. Native speakers review care phrases and regional variants; retain literal user-selected meaning. | TouchChat/Avaz/Weave localization; adding a language name is not enough. |
| R11 | P1 | **Portable personal profiles and care handover.** Separate person profile from home/hospital/family preset. Include preferred access, settling time, yes/no signal and backup method in an optional printable communication passport. Support multiple isolated local profiles, explicit export contents and shared-device reset. | Speech Assistant profiles and CoughDrop continuity. Current preset selection is not multi-person isolation. |
| R12 | P1 | **Open transfer.** Add OBF/OBZ import/export for supported boards and media while preserving licenses/attribution. Round-trip a test board through a second compatible tool; warn about unsupported actions rather than silently discarding them. Keep export free. | Cboard pricing and the [Open Board Format](https://www.openboardformat.org/) ecosystem. Reach currently exports its own JSON. |
| R13 | P1 | **Caregiver changes without interrupting speech.** Draft phrase edits, preview and apply deliberately; protect against accidental edits. A returning helper can read the communication passport and restore a known setup. No unsolicited logging of private conversations. | CoughDrop team workflows and partner coaching. Start with local edits; remote editing requires a separate consent/security design. |
| R14 | P1 | **Work with external accessibility.** Test standard pointer/click and keyboard events from OS gaze and real switches; allow Reach's camera to stay off. Prevent double dwell activation. Publish a device/browser/access-method matrix including camera conflicts. | OS tools and OptiKey are potential complements. A browser-only app should not promise system mouse control. |
| R15 | P2 | **Coarse gaze or gesture selection.** Research left/right zones, row/column selection or deliberate gestures as alternatives to precise coordinates. This may help some people, not everyone. |
| R16 | P2 | **Optional family/caregiver connection.** Separate “speak here” from “send.” For remote requests show pending, delivered and acknowledged independently; test disconnects and recipient absence. | R17 | P2 | **Optional AI assistance under user control.** Suggest phrase drafts or caregiver translation drafts; user chooses exact speech. Never infer consent, symptoms or intended answers from eye movements. Core speech and stored phrases must work without an AI service. | Competitors already offer prediction and AI features. Useful AI reduces effort while preserving authorship. |

## How to compare actual usefulness

Use AsTeRICS Grid and Cboard as the closest browser-board comparisons, Speech Assistant for adult conversation, and OS access with a suitable AAC app for alternative input. Specialist dedicated devices are a separate comparison cohort, with hardware and support costs recorded rather than treating software prices as whole-system prices.

Ask participants to complete the same chosen tasks on appropriate systems: say yes/no, request repositioning, clarify a location, correct an unintended selection, express an unprepared family message, pause/resume, and recover after repositioning. Offer the same assistance and familiarization opportunities; vary trial order to reduce learning effects. People should only trial methods they can comfortably use and can stop whenever they choose.

Record intended messages completed, time to first message, selections per message, unintended spoken messages, correction time, helper interventions, reported effort and fatigue, and successful offline restart. Report each person's outcomes and access configuration, including failures; an average must not conceal people for whom the system does not work. These are proposed evaluation measures, not established clinical thresholds or a completed benchmark.

The first development priority should be R19 and R20 plus R07 real-person evidence. Next come R12 open transfer, R18 auditory/visual choice, and completion of R09/R10 expression and localization. Keep advanced AI and remote messaging behind these foundations.

## Phrase-pack additions

These are proposed messages to review with users and fluent care partners, not medical instructions.

| Home care | Hospital care | Family communication |
|---|---|---|
| Please help me turn onto my side. | Please stop and explain. | I want to tell you something. |
| My mouth is dry. | I do not understand. | Ask me one question at a time. |
| I need help reaching something. | Please tell me what happens next. | Please give me time to answer. |
| Please adjust the light. | I need an interpreter. | That is not what I meant. |
| I want some privacy. | I want my family here. | I disagree. |
| I want to choose when we do this. | I need to describe where it hurts. | Tell me about your day. |
| Please check my communication device. | I need my communication device positioned. | Can we look at photos together? |
| I want to try doing this myself. | I have another question. | I want to talk about something else. |

Universal messages should remain reachable: Yes, No, Help, Stop, Wait, Not sure, Wrong selection, and Something else. They need not all occupy a crowded permanent toolbar: placement should match the person's target size and access method.

## Suggested delivery sequence

1. Build R01–R06 as one coherent access/recovery release; perform R07 alongside development, not after a public reliability claim.
2. Add structured care messages, family media/composition and bilingual display (R08–R10), with human vocabulary review.
3. Add profile handover, open transfer and validated external input support (R11–R14).
4. Trial coarse gaze, remote connection and optional AI separately (R15–R17), each with its own failure and consent tests.

The leading success measure should be whether a person can independently communicate an intended message and correct an error with acceptable effort. Tile count, language count and camera-model sophistication are supporting measures.

## Limits and next evidence needed

No claim is made that all competing apps have been identified or that any product is universally best. Additional regional/pediatric systems, switches, mounting hardware, and specialist devices need separate evaluation if they become relevant to a user's access needs. Exact procurement costs, support contracts, funding and hospital integration were not audited. This report proposes requirements; it does not implement them or establish Reach as clinically validated.
